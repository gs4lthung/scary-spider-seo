What is Off Page SEO? (And How to Compare With On Page)

Off page SEO includes all the actions you take outside of your own website to impact your rankings. When you compare with on page SEO, the main difference is control. On page SEO is about optimizing your own content, HTML, and site architecture. Off page is about convincing others to vouch for you. If you are new to these terms, start with our beginner's guide to the three SEO pillars.

Think of it like opening a restaurant. On page SEO is the food quality, the menu design, and the cleanliness of the dining room. Off page SEO is the food critic writing a stellar review in the local newspaper. You need both to survive.

Example: A local plumber has a lightning-fast, perfectly optimized website. However, a competitor with a mediocre website outranks them because the competitor has links from the local chamber of commerce and the town newspaper.

Actions you can take right now:

  • Run a backlink audit on your own domain using your preferred SEO tool.

  • Identify your top three competitors and review their referring domains.

  • List the industry organizations where your competitors are mentioned but you are not.

Earning Backlinks That Drive Authorization

Links are the currency of the web. However, search engines have become incredibly smart at ignoring manipulative link building. You do not need thousands of links. You need links that provide real authorization to your brand.

Quality always beats quantity. A link from a trusted, high-traffic site in your specific niche passes significant ranking power. A hundred links from automated web directories will likely do nothing or actively harm your site.

Example: Instead of submitting your site to 50 random startup directories, you write a guest column for a major software blog in your industry. That single contextual link carries massive weight.

Actions you can take right now:

  • Find broken links on industry blogs and offer your relevant content as a replacement. A crawler like Scary Spider SEO can surface them quickly.

  • Look for unlinked brand mentions. If a site mentions your company but does not link to you, send a polite email asking for a link.

  • Avoid buying links from massive email outreach lists.

Using Digital PR to Build Brand Trust

Digital PR is the modern evolution of link building. It involves creating newsworthy content or providing expert commentary to journalists. This earns you placements on highly authoritative news sites and industry publications.

Journalists are always looking for data, expert quotes, and unique perspectives. When you provide these, they reward you with brand visibility and valuable backlinks. Niche online communities are another place reporters find sources, which we cover in our guide to offpage SEO with social groups.

A few years back, we were managing SEO for a client holding down the number two spot for a highly competitive regional keyword. To build the kind of authentic authority search engines trust, we surveyed 500 local users about their biggest industry frustration and turned the findings into a simple, one-page infographic. We emailed the key data points to a handful of local journalists. Within three days, a major national news outlet republished our chart and cited our client with a contextual link. That single piece of genuine research built more real-world trust than six months of cold outreach ever could. Example: A cybersecurity company publishes original data on the most common password mistakes. They pitch this data to tech reporters, resulting in dozens of high-quality editorial links.

Actions you can take right now:

  • Sign up for platforms that connect journalists with sources, like Connectively or Qwoted.

  • Spend 15 minutes a day answering relevant queries in your niche.

  • Publish an original data study or survey on your blog to use as pitch material.

Optimizing Local Citations and Profiles

If you run a local business or have a physical headquarters, local off-site signals are mandatory. Search engines use citations to verify your existence and legitimacy.

A citation is any online mention of your business name, address, and phone number. Consistency across the web builds confidence in your business data. Inconsistent data confuses search engines and hurts your local rankings.

Example: A dental clinic ensures their address and phone number match exactly on their Google Business Profile, Apple Maps, Yelp, and the local medical board directory.

Actions you can take right now:

  • Claim and optimize your Google Business Profile completely, following Google's guidelines for business profiles.

  • Use a citation audit tool to find mismatched phone numbers or old addresses across the web.

  • Submit your business to the top 10 industry-specific directories in your field.

The bottom line

Great content alone is rarely enough to dominate search results. You need the web to validate your expertise. Off page SEO is not a one-time project. It is a continuous process of building relationships, earning authorization, and proving your value to the wider internet.

Start small. Focus on earning one high-quality mention or link this month. As you consistently apply these strategies, you will see your rankings rise and your traffic stabilize.
